## 1. Startup Cost Categories

### One-Time Setup Costs
| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Legal & registration | Company incorporation, trademark, contracts |
| Equipment & hardware | Computers, machinery, POS systems, vehicles |
| Facility | Deposit, fit-out, renovation |
| Technology setup | Website, app development, software licenses |
| Branding | Logo, brand identity, marketing materials |
| Initial inventory | Stock, raw materials, packaging |

### Ongoing Monthly Costs (Fixed)
| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Rent / co-working | Office, retail, warehouse space |
| Salaries | Full-time and part-time staff |
| Software subscriptions | SaaS tools, hosting, CRM, accounting |
| Insurance | Liability, property, health |
| Loan repayments | If applicable |

### Variable Costs (Scale with Revenue)
- Cost of goods sold (COGS) — materials, manufacturing, packaging
- Payment processing fees (typically 1.5–3.5%)
- Shipping and fulfillment
- Sales commissions
- Customer support costs

## 2. Phased Budget Planning

### Phase 1: Pre-Launch (Months -3 to 0)
- Legal setup and registration
- MVP product or service development
- Initial branding and website
- Early customer discovery and validation
- Hire only essential roles

### Phase 2: Launch (Month 1–2)
- First marketing spend (focus on 1–2 channels only)
- Initial inventory or service capacity
- Operations tools (POS, CRM, accounting)
- 3-month cash reserve (critical)

### Phase 3: Early Growth (Months 3–6)
- Double down on what's working in marketing
- First team expansions if revenue supports it
- System upgrades as volume increases
- Begin reinvesting margin into growth

**Rule of thumb**: Hold 3–6 months of operating expenses in reserve at all times.

## 3. Revenue Projection Methods

### Bottom-Up Projection (More Accurate)
```
Weekly customers × Average order value × 52 weeks = Year 1 Revenue
```
Build from realistic unit assumptions, not from top-down market percentages.

### Cohort-Based (For Subscriptions)
```
Month 1: 10 customers × $50/month = $500 MRR
Month 2: +15 new, 2 churn → 23 customers × $50 = $1,150 MRR
...
```
Track Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) and churn rate separately.

### Conservative / Base / Optimistic Scenarios
Always model 3 scenarios:
- **Conservative** (50% of base): What if acquisition is slow?
- **Base** (your honest estimate): Most likely outcome
- **Optimistic** (150% of base): What if things go well?

Use conservative scenario for cash planning, base for investor presentations.

## 4. Break-Even Analysis

```
Break-Even (Units) = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit)
Break-Even (Revenue) = Fixed Costs ÷ Gross Margin %
```

**Example**:
- Fixed costs: $5,000/month
- Price per unit: $25
- Variable cost per unit: $10
- Contribution margin: $15
- Break-even: 5,000 ÷ 15 = **334 units/month**

Track time-to-break-even as a key milestone. Healthy range: 6–18 months for most businesses.

## 5. Cash Flow Planning

### Cash Flow ≠ Profit
A business can be profitable on paper but run out of cash (e.g., if customers pay late).

### Cash Flow Warning Signs
- [ ] Receivables growing faster than revenue (customers aren't paying on time)
- [ ] Inventory growing without revenue growth (overstock, slow-moving)
- [ ] Monthly burn rate increasing faster than MRR growth
- [ ] Running less than 60 days of cash runway
- [ ] Taking on debt to cover operating costs (not investment)

### 13-Week Cash Flow Forecast
Build a weekly cash flow model covering the next 13 weeks:
- Opening cash balance
- Expected inflows (sales, payments, loans)
- Expected outflows (rent, payroll, suppliers, subscriptions)
- Closing cash balance

Update weekly. This is the most important financial tool in Year 1.

## 6. Funding Options

| Option | Best For | Tradeoffs |
|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrapping** | Service businesses, low capex | No dilution; limited scale speed |
| **Friends & Family** | Pre-revenue stage | Relationship risk if things go wrong |
| **Bank Loans** | Asset-backed businesses | Requires collateral; monthly repayment |
| **Angel Investors** | Early-stage, high-growth potential | Dilution; investor expectations |
| **Venture Capital** | High-growth, scalable startups | Heavy dilution; pressure for fast growth |
| **Grants / Government programs** | SMEs, tech, agriculture, social enterprise | Non-dilutive; competitive process |
| **Crowdfunding** | Consumer products, community-driven | Marketing effort required |
| **Revenue-Based Financing** | SaaS or subscription businesses | Repay as % of revenue; no equity lost |

### Vietnam-Specific Funding Sources
- National Technology Innovation Fund (NATIF / NAFOSTED)
- Vietnam Silicon Valley (VSV) accelerator
- Do Ventures, Jungle Ventures, 500 Startups Southeast Asia
- VCCI SME support programs
- Provincial industrial development funds

## 7. Financial Benchmarks by Industry Subagent

### Fintech / Payments
- CAC: $20–$150 (consumer), $500–$2,000 (SME)
- LTV:CAC ratio target: ≥3:1
- Gross margin: 50–80% (software-based), 5–20% (transaction-based)
- Churn: <2%/month for lending; <5%/month for payment apps

### EdTech
- CAC: $30–$200 (B2C), $500–$5,000 (B2B school/enterprise)
- Gross margin: 60–85% (digital content), 30–50% (live instruction)
- Completion rate: >60% = healthy engagement
- Annual contract renewal rate target: >80%

### E-commerce / Retail
- COGS: 30–50% of revenue
- Gross margin: 40–60% (branded), 10–25% (reseller/marketplace)
- CAC via paid social: $5–$50 depending on product category
- Return rate: <10% = healthy; >20% = review product quality or description

### SaaS / B2B Software
- Gross margin target: >70%
- Monthly churn target: <2%
- CAC payback period: <12 months
- Rule of 40: (Revenue growth % + Profit margin %) ≥ 40 = healthy SaaS

### F&B / Restaurant
- Food cost (COGS): 28–35% of revenue
- Labor cost: 25–35% of revenue
- Occupancy cost: ≤10% of revenue
- Net profit margin: 3–9% (industry average)
- Break-even occupancy: typically 60–70% of seating capacity

### Healthcare / MedTech
- Reimbursement cycle: 30–120 days (plan cash flow carefully)
- Gross margin (medical devices): 50–70%
- Regulatory cost: Budget $50K–$500K+ for approval processes
- Sales cycle (hospital/enterprise): 6–18 months

## 8. Key Financial Metrics to Track

| Metric | Definition | Review Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| MRR / ARR | Monthly/Annual Recurring Revenue | Monthly |
| Gross Margin | (Revenue − COGS) ÷ Revenue | Monthly |
| CAC | Total sales & marketing spend ÷ new customers | Monthly |
| LTV | Average revenue per customer × average lifespan | Quarterly |
| LTV:CAC ratio | Should be ≥3:1 | Quarterly |
| Churn rate | % customers lost per month | Monthly |
| Burn rate | Net cash spent per month | Weekly |
| Runway | Cash on hand ÷ monthly burn rate | Weekly |
| Break-even | See Section 4 | Monthly |
| Cash conversion cycle | Days to convert investment to cash | Monthly |
